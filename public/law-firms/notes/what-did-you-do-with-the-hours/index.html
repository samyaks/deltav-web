What did you do with the hours?

AI made a repeated matter shorter. The hours that came off it went somewhere. A lower fee, a higher margin, or a write-off. Which door did yours take?

For the people who own the seats, the knowledge program, or the fee

Lawyers using the tool is no longer the interesting claim. Firms can show logins, assigned seats, and a training completion rate. Those numbers answered last year's question.

This year's question is the matter you already do every week. A lease review. An employment agreement. A closing checklist. It used to take a known number of hours. On the matters where AI actually ran, it takes fewer. Those hours used to be on a bill.

Three doors

The saved time leaves the firm through one of three doors.

  1. 01

    The fee comes down.

    You quote a fixed or capped fee from the new hours, and you can still say what margin that leaves.

  2. 02

    The margin goes up.

    The fee stays. The lawyer time drops. The gain is inside the matter, and someone can point to it.

  3. 03

    The write-off takes them.

    The bill is still built on the old hours, and a partner cuts it later. Or the work got so short that nobody entered the time at all.

Enablement belongs in this picture too. A lawyer who can take a matter the firm used to decline is a real gain, and it will not show up as fewer hours on an old task. Name that new work. Then come back to the matter type you already price, because that is where a shorter draft changes the fee, the margin, or the write-off.

One matter type is enough

You do not need a firm-wide study to join this. Pick one type you do over and over. Three numbers:

  • Hours before, from matters you already closed.
  • Hours on the matters where the AI actually ran.
  • The door: a new fee, a margin you can defend, or a write-off.

If the second number is almost the same as the first, that is an answer. The tool is not in that workflow. You learn where a seat is doing nothing, and where you should not promise a client a faster fixed fee.
