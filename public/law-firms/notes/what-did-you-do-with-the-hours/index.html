The bill still assumes the old hours

Firms can show who logged in. On the work they already sell, few can say whether a shorter draft became a lower fee, a wider margin or a write-off.

Law firms have spent two years proving that lawyers will open the software. Pilots, seat assignments and compulsory training have made that demonstration easy. A dashboard of logins settles an argument that is now finished. It says nothing about the bill.

Take a commercial lease review, the sort of job a firm prices from memory because it has done so many. For the illustration on this site we use a made-up file: 14.2 hours before, 8.6 on the matters where a lawyer actually used the tool. These are not a client’s numbers. The arithmetic is the point. Five and a half hours used to be time the firm could sell. They are still somewhere in the file.

A firm that has been paying attention has already chosen, whether or not anyone wrote the choice down. It cut the fee, and can say what margin remains. It held the fee, and the gain is sitting in the matter for anyone who lays the hours next to the bill. Or the invoice is still drawn up as though the review took fourteen hours, and the difference is written off at the end of the month, or never recorded, because the draft was finished before anyone started a timer.

Put that question to the partner who sets fees and the comparison is often missing from both reports. Innovation can show adoption, finance can show realization, and neither report contains the matter. The people who bought the tools and the people who defend the fee are frequently not in the same meeting, though a shorter first draft changes both of their jobs.

Some of the value will never appear as fewer hours on an old task. A lawyer takes work the firm used to send out. A client who would have refused a full review now asks for one. That is new business, and it needs its own line. It is not a reason to ignore the work the firm already sells every week, which is where a shorter draft runs into a fee someone has to stand behind.

A comparison in which the hours barely move is worth having too. It means the tool is not in that workflow. Renewal becomes a smaller argument, and so does any promise to a client that this particular job has become quicker.

What is worth comparing is modest: one matter type the firm already knows by heart, the hours before the tool and the hours on the files where it was actually used, and a sentence on whether the fee moved.
